# T-5.4 — bound approval and provenance

2026-11-21. Fourth task of the agent-factory program (`docs/agent-factory-program-plan.md`). T-5.3 could derive an authority from a definition; this task makes that derivation **mean something**: one human approval that covers the definition, its derived policy and the session key together, with a record that cannot be quietly rewritten afterwards. It completes the C3 part of ADR-006 — a platform that governs agents — without any model involved. No contract changed.

## What changed in practice

**One screen for the agent, not just its limits** (`domain/agent_approval.py`). `describe_agent` shows the name, purpose, model and sampling parameters, risk tier, funded budget, provenance identifier, the tools with the catalog's own summaries, the agent's instructions in full, and then the entire T-2.6 policy screen beneath. The instructions carry a caveat stating the thing that keeps I-11 true: *they grant it nothing; the limits below are the whole of what it may do.* `render_agent_text` prints all of it.

**A fingerprint over the triple.** `agent_fingerprint` hashes the definition digest, the policy digest and the session public key together. An approval carries that fingerprint, the warning keys, the approver and the time. `approve_agent` refuses unless the fingerprint matches exactly, every raised warning is acknowledged and no other, the approver is the owner named in the definition, and the approval carries its time. T-2.7's rotation fingerprint bound two of the three; this binds all three.

**The screen refuses a policy that is not the definition's.** Before anything is shown, `describe_agent` checks that the policy's caps, window and end time are the definition's and that it is payments-only. Without that check, a screen could show one agent's text above another agent's limits.

**An append-only provenance log** (`application/provenance_log.py`). Each entry carries a sequence number, the agent, the event, the time, a few short plain-text details, optionally the definition document, and a digest over all of that **including the previous entry's digest**. `verify` recomputes the whole chain, so an altered entry, a renumbered entry or a broken link is detectable. `recorded_definition` reproduces the definition from the record and refuses it unless it still matches the digest recorded beside it.

**The drill now goes through the real path.** For each fleet agent the provisioning derives the policy, records the derivation with the definition, builds the agent screen, has the owner approve it, records the approval, installs the modules, issues the policy, funds the account and records the installation. Nothing is installed except under an approval.

## Design decisions (please review)

1. **The approver must be the owner named in the definition.** This is I-9 in code: a generated agent's authority is approved by the person the definition says owns it, not by whoever happens to be driving the ceremony.
2. **The fingerprint is 16 bytes, shown in four groups.** Long enough that two agents in a fleet cannot collide by accident, short enough to read aloud against a device.
3. **The screen carries the definition object.** It is an in-process object, not a wire format, so the approval can return a complete `ApprovedAgent` without re-plumbing the definition through the caller.
4. **The chain covers the previous digest.** That is what makes "append-only" verifiable rather than aspirational: two logs with identical entries but different histories have different digests, and the test asserts exactly that.
5. **The log is keyed by agent identifier**, with the provenance identifier kept as a detail, because the drill and the fleet ledger already speak in agent identifiers.
6. **Three events only** — derived, approved, installed. Declined and retired belong to the lifecycle state machine in T-5.8, and I did not add enum values nothing uses.
7. **The payee list is not cross-checked against the definition** in the screen, because the definition names payees by registry identifier and the policy by address; resolving one to the other is derivation's job, and derivation is the only thing that produces a policy here.

## Mapping to the acceptance criteria

| T-5.4 acceptance criterion | Where it is proven |
|---|---|
| An approval cannot be replayed onto another definition | `test_an_approval_cannot_be_replayed_onto_another_definition` |
| An approval cannot be replayed onto another key | `test_an_approval_cannot_be_replayed_onto_another_session_key` |
| A changed definition needs a fresh approval | `test_a_changed_definition_needs_a_fresh_approval` |
| The provenance record of an approved agent is complete and reproducible from its digest | `test_the_recorded_definition_is_reproduced_and_checked_against_its_digest`, `test_a_definition_that_does_not_match_its_digest_is_refused`, and the drill check |
| A definition whose text contains invisible or bidirectional characters is refused | T-5.3's `test_a_malformed_definition_is_refused`, which covers a zero-width space and a bidirectional override through the shared plain-text rule; such a definition cannot be constructed, so it can never reach a screen |

## Evidence

- **Python: 657 tests pass** (623 before, 34 new). `ruff`, `ruff format` and `mypy --strict` clean on source and tests. Solidity is untouched at 218.
- **Drill: 34 of 34** (33 before), on two consecutive runs of the final code. The new check verifies the provenance chain, requires each agent to have a derivation, an approval and an installation recorded in that order, reproduces the definition from the record and checks it against its digest, recomputes the fingerprint from the **recorded** definition, the derived policy and the key the validator actually holds, and requires that recomputed value to be both the approval the owner gave and the one the record names.
- **Mutations: 24 at unit level, all caught** — every refusal in `approve_agent`, each of the three parts of the fingerprint dropped in turn, a shortened fingerprint, the acknowledgement set emptied, each of the four policy-belongs-to-the-definition checks, the missing-key check, and in the log the link check, the digest check, the definition-digest check, the derivation-needs-a-definition rule, the identifier and time checks, the detail rules, the head, and the previous digest removed from the sealed body.
- **Two drill-level mutations**, both caught by the new check: recording a fingerprint the agent does not run under ("agent-one recorded an approval it does not run under") and never recording the approval at all ("agent-one has the record [derived, installed]").
- **Rendering the screen was worth it again.** It showed the whole-session maximum as 2 ETH against a 1 ETH window cap, which is correct and surprising — see the first risk below — and it showed an unclear `Record:` label, now `Provenance:`.

## Risks and limits

- **The clock-skew margin can double the worst-case total.** Derivation starts a session 300 seconds before the definition's start, so a one-day session spans one day and five minutes, which crosses a second rolling window: the honest whole-session maximum becomes twice the window cap, and the screen says so. A deployment should either size sessions as whole window multiples or accept the stated figure. I left the arithmetic visible rather than hiding it with an adjustment.
- **The approver is a scripted stand-in in the drill.** It renders the screen, acknowledges every warning and returns the owner's address. Whether a person understands the screen is still untested, and there is still no graphical surface.
- **No declined path is exercised end to end.** `approve_agent` refuses everything it should, and the drill proves nothing is installed without a recorded approval, but a declined agent is not run through the drill; that belongs with the lifecycle in T-5.8.
- **The log is in memory and unsigned.** The chain makes tampering detectable within a process; it does not survive a restart, and nothing signs an entry, so a hostile process could rebuild a consistent chain from scratch. Durability and signing are open.
- **Approvals are not stored.** `ApprovedAgent` lives in memory for the ceremony; nothing can later prove to a third party that the owner approved a particular fingerprint, beyond the log's own record.
- **The registry and catalog are still unauthenticated** (T-5.3's limit): whoever can call `approve` on the payee registry can add a payee, and the owner's approval covers the resulting agent rather than the registry change itself.
- **Still local:** simulated attestation, development keys, Alto with safe mode off, no Base Sepolia, unaudited.
