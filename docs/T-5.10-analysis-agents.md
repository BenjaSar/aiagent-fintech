# T-5.10 — the analysis agent type

2026-11-21. Tenth and last task of the agent-factory program (`docs/agent-factory-program-plan.md`). Its gate was a decision, not a drill check: ADR-006 D-5 allowed payment agents only. `docs/ADR-007-stage-2-agent-types.md` was written as that extension and **accepted as proposed by the project owner on 2026-11-21**, which opens exactly one type — E-2's **analysis** agent, which holds no authority at all. This task builds it. No contract changed, and no agent gained a new way to move value.

## What changed in practice

**A second agent type, and the artifacts learned to have none.** `AgentType.ANALYSIS` joins `PAYMENT`, and `AUTHORITY_FREE_TYPES` names the types that hold no authority. An `AgentDefinition` of such a type declares **no payee, no asset and no budget** — `budget` is now `Budget | None` — and one that declares any of them is refused. A paying type without a budget is refused too, which was previously impossible to express. The same split runs through `TaskSpecification`, so an owner cannot write a read-only task that carries a ceiling.

**The document keeps its shape.** `budget` serialises as `null` for a reader; no field was added or removed, so `SCHEMA_VERSION` stays 1, and a reader that round-trips through its document is byte-identical by digest. A build that did not know about null budgets fails closed on the unpacking, which is what E-7 asks for.

**Three gates say no, in three places.** The synthesis gate refuses a proposal that names a value-moving tool for an authority-free type, which is the case that matters when an owner's task specification mistakenly allows one (a tool outside the specification is already refused). `derive_session_policy` refuses an authority-free definition by design and says so — *analysis agents hold no authority, so no session policy is derived for them* — rather than as a stage 1 limitation. And `describe_agent` refuses to present a reader that carries a policy or a session key.

**An approval screen that states the absence.** A reader's screen has **no policy screen**, requires no acknowledgements, shows its budget as *none - this agent holds no account and can move no value*, and ends with a block that says in words: it has no account, no session key and no spending policy, and approving it grants nothing that can move value. Its fingerprint binds its **definition digest alone**, because there is no policy digest and no key to bind.

**Tool kinds are now classified, not assumed** (E-3). `AUTHORITY_KINDS` and `READING_KINDS` partition `ToolKind`, `authority_tools` reports the authority-bearing tools of a resolved set, and `domain/authority_order.py` asks that question instead of comparing against `ToolKind.PAYMENT` by name. A test asserts the two sets still partition the enum, so adding a kind without classifying it fails the suite rather than slipping past the monotonic rule. ADR-007 §2 found that blind spot; this closes it before anything could walk into it.

**A reader's own evaluation bank.** `evaluate` branches on the type. A reader is judged by three checks — it holds no paying tool, it declares no payee, asset or budget, and **no policy was derived for it** — and two scenarios: it reads the approved payees and the state of one payment and reports; and the same definition driven by the *payment* decider is refused at the tool boundary, with nothing written to the journal and **its account never touched**. The account in both scenarios is a stub that records any attempt to read a balance or submit a payment, so "it cannot pay" is measured, not assumed.

**A runtime that can be asked to read.** The runtime's task is now `PaymentTask | ReportTask`; `ScriptedReportDecider` lists the payees, reads one payment's status and reports; and `ScriptedPaymentDecider` refuses a report task outright. The analysis agent is given the fleet's payment journal by design — reporting on payments is its purpose — and holds no authority, so reading is all it can do.

**Authoring, with nothing to provision.** `FleetProvisioning.author_analysis_agent` runs the same sequence as a payment agent minus everything on chain: synthesise, record, evaluate, record, approve, record, activate. There is no account, no salt, no DKG, no enclave tenancy, no funding and no install, and the fleet ledger is untouched. Its provenance chain is three entries — generated, evaluated, approved — and the chain still verifies.

## Deviations from the plan

1. **The catalog gained no tools.** ADR-007 §3(A) said a reader would reuse the catalog and "plausibly" add a market price, a transfer history or a fleet-wide balance. None of those exists to read: a price needs an oracle (an IG-13-shaped dependency) and a fleet-wide balance needs a reader-visible view that is not built. The analysis type therefore draws `list_payees` and `get_payment_status` from the reviewed stage 1 catalog and nothing else.
2. **`get_balance` is not a reader's tool.** It reads *the agent account's own balance*, and a reader has no account. A reader that named it would fail its own evaluation, because the stub account would record the attempt. That is the correct answer rather than a limitation, but it does mean "read-only" is narrower here than it sounds.
3. **The analysis agent is not renewed or halted.** It is not a `ProvisionedAgent`, so the expiry scheduler, the halt switch and the enclave tenancies do not see it. Its window is in its definition and nothing acts on it. An authority-free agent has nothing to halt in the authority plane; stopping one is a matter of not running it, which is not modelled.

## Design decisions (please review)

1. **One artifact, two shapes, not two artifacts.** A reader is an `AgentDefinition` whose authority fields are empty, not a new type with its own digest, provenance and approval path. The cost is optionality in three places (`budget`, the presentation's policy, the screen's policy screen); the gain is that one approval artifact, one fingerprint function and one provenance chain cover both types.
2. **`SCHEMA_VERSION` stays 1.** No field was added or removed. A null budget makes an older reader fail closed on unpacking rather than misread a definition, which is the property E-7 cares about; a version bump would have invalidated nothing real, since no definition is persisted outside a run.
3. **A reader's fingerprint binds its definition alone.** There is no policy and no key, so the triple in I-9 has one member. I-9 itself is vacuous for this type — nothing becomes an on-chain authority — and the screen says so in words rather than leaving a reader to infer it from an empty limits section.
4. **The type rule in the synthesis gate runs after the generic rules.** A reader that proposes a payee or a cap is refused by the rules that already existed (that payee is not in the task, that asset has no ceiling), with their clearer messages; the type rule exists for the one case they cannot catch, which is a mistaken specification that allows a paying tool.
5. **Evaluation measures "cannot pay" with a recording stub.** The reader's balance reader and payment sink are one object that records any call and settles nothing. A scenario that merely checked the summary would pass even if the agent had reached its account.
6. **The reader reads the fleet's journal.** Its purpose is to report on payments, so it is given the journal rather than a per-agent slice. It can therefore read the status of any payment whose idempotency key it is told, which is a read of fleet data by design and is worth saying out loud.

## Evidence

- **Python: 954 tests pass** (922 before, 32 new: 19 for the type end to end, 6 for the definition and its document, 3 for the report decider and task, 2 for the kind partition, 2 for the monotonic rule). `ruff`, `ruff format` and `mypy --strict` clean over 155 files. Solidity is untouched at 218, `forge fmt --check` clean.
- **Drill: 46 of 46** (45 before), on two consecutive runs of the final code. The new check reports: a generator that proposed a paying tool for a reader was refused; the approved reader holds no account, no session key, no payee, no asset and no budget; showing it with a key is refused; its fingerprint binds its definition alone; deriving a policy for it is refused; its screen says so; it read the fleet's payees and one payment and reported; and a payment task was refused at the tool boundary with no account touched.
- **Mutations: 24 at unit level, all caught** on the first pass, across the kind partition, both artifacts, the document, the gate, derivation, the approval path, the renderer, the monotonic rule, the evaluation branch and both deciders.
- **Three drill-level mutations, all caught** after one addition. A screen that stops saying the reader holds nothing gives *the screen did not say it holds no authority*; a gate that lets a reader hold a paying tool gives *a reader was given a tool that can pay*; and an approval path that accepts a reader carrying a session key gives *a reader was shown with a session key*.

### What the mutations taught me

The third drill mutation survived at first. Removing the guard that refuses to present a reader with a policy or a key changed nothing in the drill, because the drill's own composition passes neither — the control is there for a mistaken caller, and the drill was only exercising the correct one. Rather than leave it to the unit tests, the drill now asks the approval path to show the authored reader *with* a session key and requires the refusal. That makes the check adversarial about its own composition, which is the point: the question is not whether the happy path works but whether a wrong caller is stopped.

## Risks and limits

- **"Holds no authority" is a property of the authority plane, not of the agent's effect.** A reader can still mislead. Its output can inform a human, and nothing stops a person or a later system from acting on a report that an injected payee label or purpose text shaped. The controls that matter for this type are T-5.6's injection suite and T-5.7's isolation walk, not anything on chain, and the type's risk is informational rather than financial.
- **Nothing consumes a reader's report.** T-5.9's trace can show a reader acting in a sequence, but no payment agent takes a report as an input, and ADR-007 §6 deliberately left that composition question open. It is the obvious next one, and it is where a reader's output would start to matter.
- **The reader's journal access is fleet-wide.** Any payment whose key it is given can be read. In a fleet with several owners that would need a slice; with one owner it is the point of the type.
- **An authority-free approval is a weaker thing to approve.** The owner approves a definition digest, not a triple, so two readers that differ only in their prompt are two approvals, but there is no on-chain artifact to check an approval against afterwards. The provenance chain is the only record that a reader was approved at all.
- **Three types remain closed** on the gates ADR-007 names: treasury behind E-4 (the treasury account never holds a session key), trading behind E-5 (T-V2, T-V3 and a price source, because it would turn off the hook's payments-only policy) and x402 behind E-6 (T-3.3 and a custody re-review).
- **Still local:** simulated attestation, development keys, Alto with safe mode off, no Base Sepolia, unaudited, and no model — the proposer and both deciders are deterministic stand-ins behind ports.
